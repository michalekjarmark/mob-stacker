# Breeding, shearing & drops

## Breeding a stack

With `enableStackBreeding` on (the default), **feeding a stacked animal breeds its members in pairs**,
without taking them out of the stack:

- it takes **one food item per member**: 16 cows fed 16 wheat give 8 calves,
- a partial feed is remembered, so food is never wasted,
- the members that bred go on vanilla's ~5-minute cooldown; the rest can still be bred,
- animals vanilla would not let breed (an untamed wolf, for one) are not bred here either.

`breedOnePerClick` feeds one member per click instead of as many as the food in your hand allows.

**The babies arrive as one baby stack** (a young `Cow x8`), grow up together and then join the adult
stack. Feeding a baby stack speeds up its growth, scaled to its size.

Loose babies stack with each other too: farm animals of about the same age
(`enableAnimalBabyStacking`), and babies that do not age, such as baby zombies
(`enableHostileBabyStacking`). Both are on by default.

> [!NOTE]
> Wolves, cats and horses have their own right-click behaviour and breed the vanilla way.

## Shearing and milking

With `stackedHarvest` on (the default), **a stack gives one mob's worth per member**:

- shearing a stack of 16 sheep gives 16 sheep's worth of wool and costs 16 points of shear durability,
- a stack of mooshrooms gives mushrooms for each and turns into a stack of cows,
- a stack of snow golems gives a carved pumpkin each,
- milking a stack of cows fills as many buckets as you hold, one per cow.

A **dispenser** with shears does exactly what a player's shears do.

With `stackedHarvest` **off**, a stack gives what a single mob would: shears take **one** animal out of
the stack and shear that one, so the rest keep their wool for the next click.

> [!NOTE]
> Shear state is part of what a sheep stack has to share, so sheared and woolly sheep form separate
> stacks. When a stack of sheared sheep eats grass, the whole stack grows its wool back at once.

## Eggs and other gifts

With `multiplyGiftDrops` on (the default), **what mobs drop on their own comes from every mob in a
stack**: a stack of chickens lays an egg for each chicken, and a stack of baby turtles leaves a scute
for each turtle when it grows up. Off, a stack drops these like a single mob.

## Drops and experience

Killing a big stack would normally litter the ground with an item per drop per mob and a swarm of tiny
experience orbs. With `compactDrops` and `compactExperience` (both on), **a stack's death drops come
out merged into as few full item stacks as possible, and its experience as one orb**.

Nothing is created or lost: the same loot and the same experience, in fewer entities. Items that differ
(enchantments, names, other data) are kept apart. Only stacked mobs are affected, whatever killed them.
