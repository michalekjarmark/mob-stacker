# Combat

By default a stack fights like a single mob: a hit that kills the top mob removes it, and the next
one takes its place. The settings below decide what happens to the rest of the stack.

## Damage overflow

With `damageOverflow` on (the default), **the damage left over from a killing blow carries on to the
mobs below**:

- every mob it kills drops its loot and experience and counts as a kill,
- a mob it does not finish is left **wounded**, not healed,
- it uses the final damage after armour and resistance, so it works for **any** source: a sword, an
  arrow, a Harming potion, magic.

Sharpness, Smite, Bane of Arthropods and Fire Aspect raise the hit, so they kill more mobs. Looting
applies to every mob killed.

## Killing the whole stack at once

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| `killWholeStackOnDeath` | Killing the top mob kills the whole stack, with loot for every mob. Overflow is not needed then. |
| `stackHealth` | The stack has one health bar, scaled to its size. Forces `killWholeStackOnDeath` on while it is on. |
| `damageWeaponPerHit` | With either of the two above, every hit wears your weapon down as if it had hit every mob in the stack (a sword by the stack's size, an axe twice that). Off by default: one hit's wear. |

## A fixed number per kill

`killBatchSize` (default 1) makes **at least that many mobs die each time a stack is killed**, each with
its own loot and experience, on top of what damage overflow reaches. For farms that want a steady
number per kill whatever the weapon. It does nothing while the whole stack dies at once.

## Stacks that hit back

With `stackOutgoingDamage` on (off by default), **a stack's melee attack hits as hard as all its mobs
together**: a stack of five zombies hits for five zombies, up to `maxDamageMultiplier` (16) times one
mob's damage. It covers vanilla's standard melee attack (zombies, husks, spiders, endermen, piglins,
vindicators and most others); special attacks such as an iron golem's fling, a slime's bounce or arrows
stay one mob's.

## Sweeping Edge

Sweeping Edge normally hurts the mobs *around* the one you hit, and a stack is one entity, so it would
do nothing. With `sweepingEdgeOverflow` on (the default) the vanilla sweep,
`1 + attack damage × level / (level + 1)`, is added to the hit:

| Level | Sweep damage |
|---|---|
| I | `1 + 0.50 × attack damage` |
| II | `1 + 0.67 × attack damage` |
| III | `1 + 0.75 × attack damage` |

Finer control:

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| `sweepingEdgePerMob` | The mob you hit takes the full blow and **every other mob takes its own sweep**, like a vanilla sweep through a crowd. The wounds add up from swing to swing, so a stack falls apart over a few swings. |
| `sweepingEdgeSingleHit` | All those sweeps go into the one hit instead, and overflow carries it down: one swing kills several mobs outright instead of wounding them all. Needs `sweepingEdgePerMob`. |
| `sweepingEdgeVanillaConditions` | Only sweep when vanilla would: a full swing, no critical hit, not sprinting, on the ground, a sword in hand. |
| `sweepingEdgeMaxKills` | At most this many mobs killed by one swing's sweep (0 = no limit). |

> [!WARNING]
> A stack stands in one spot, so a sweep strong enough to kill one healthy mob of its kind kills
> **all** of them in one swing. That is what vanilla would do to the same mobs side by side, but a big
> jump in power. `sweepingEdgeMaxKills` and `sweepingEdgeVanillaConditions` rein it in.

Overflow and sweeping are independent: overflow decides whether a killing blow's leftover goes on,
sweeping decides how much a sweep deals at all. To get one kill per hit, switch off
`sweepingEdgeOverflow` as well as `damageOverflow`.

## Damage from the world

With `environmentDamagePerMob` on (the default), **a fall, fire, lava, drowning, suffocation,
cramming, magma, a cactus, freezing, the void, poison, an explosion or a falling anvil hurts every mob
in a stack**, as it would hurt the same mobs standing loose. The ones it kills drop their own loot; the
survivors keep the wound.

So a fall-damage farm built for single mobs works with stacks: a 30-block drop kills a whole stack of
ten zombies, not one of them. A hit from a player or a mob stays one hit, with overflow carrying the
rest.

## Kill feedback

| Setting | Effect | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `stackKillActionBar` | A line above the hotbar: how many died and how many are left | on |
| `stackKillParticles` | A burst of particles that grows with the number killed | on |
| `stackKillHologram` | A short-lived floating `-N` above the mob | on |
| `killHologramColor` | The colour of that `-N` | `RED` |

The hologram is never saved with the world, so it cannot get stuck in the air after a restart.
